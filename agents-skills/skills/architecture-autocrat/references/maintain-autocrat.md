# Procedure 3, autocrat - the implementer gets no vote

The channel to propose first. The architect owns the contract and rules on every question; implementers ask and report, and propose nothing.

**Read `architect`'s procedure 3 before this file.** Its steps and its ruling contract hold in both modes, and everything below is what autocrat adds to them.

## What the architect may open, and what stays shut

**Rule from three sources only: the question as asked, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the product docs.** Between them they carry what is being asked, what the contract already settles, and what the product needs - which is all a rule is made of.

**Never open the branch a question came from, and never ask for its diff.** `architecture-md` §4 judges laundering by the order of reasoning, and an architect reading that code is asking what would make it legal.

**Treat the refusal as the mechanism, not as economy.** An architect who has seen the branch can no longer tell a rule from a description of what is already there, and the ruling then places one file instead of every later file of its kind.

**Ignore code an implementer pastes, and say in the ruling that it played no part.** Silence reads as consent, and the next question arrives with more of the branch attached.

**Say the contract cannot settle a case rather than opening the code to settle it.** A question the three sources cannot answer is a hole in the file, and the hole is the finding.

## What may enter the channel

**Accept two kinds of message and no third.** A question and a defect report are the whole channel; a design, a proposal or a choice between two shapes belongs to the democrat mode, and taking one here makes the branch the author of the contract.

| Message | Well formed when it carries |
|---|---|
| Question | the case, the layers or paths it falls between, what the asker already read in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the contradiction or the hole they hit there |
| Defect report | the rule quoted from `ARCHITECTURE.md`, what it requires of this kind of file, and the fact about the product that makes that impossible or wrong |

**Strip the options out of a question that arrives as a menu, and rule on the case underneath.** Picking from a shortlist the branch drew is choosing between its two readings, and neither of them was made from the contract.

**Ask for the case where stripping the options leaves nothing behind.** A proposal with no question under it names no boundary, so there is nothing to rule on.

**Bounce with the section that settles it, and never with the derivation.** Inverting the rule for the asker is the lookup `architect`'s gate refuses, and this mode has no counter-proposal to offer in its place.

## How a defect report is weighed

**Weigh a report as evidence about the contract, never as a proposal to accept or refuse.** The implementer is claiming that a rule the file states is unbuildable or wrong about the product, and a claim is tested rather than conceded.

**Test it by asking whether the rule fails for every file of that kind or only for the one at hand.** Every file of that kind, and the report holds. Only this one, and it is a question about where that file belongs, answered as a question.

**Ask for no code, at either outcome.** A defect report's claim is general or it is not a defect, and a general claim is checkable against the contract and the product alone.

## When repeated questions indict the contract

**Separate the two patterns by what repeats - the area or the asker.** One asker across many areas has not read the file; several askers in one area have read it and found the same gap, and only the second is a finding about the contract.

**Widen the fix once an area has drawn its second question.** The blocked implementer still gets their ruling; what changes is that the edit covers the class those questions circle, rather than the instance that happened to arrive last.
